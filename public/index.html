Mission Briefing

You've just logged into the Cyber Threat Consensus Board — a live Security Operations Center (SOC) social-deduction game. One connected analyst is secretly the Insider Threat. Everyone else just wants to triage three incidents and figure out who it is before the shift ends.

How a shift plays out

  1. Wait in the lobby until at least 3 analysts have connected, then anyone can click Start Shift.
  2. The server secretly assigns exactly one connected analyst as the Insider Threat. Everyone else is an Analyst. Your role banner shows yours and only yours.
  3. Three threats go active, one after another, each with a 20-second clock. Drag Severity and Likelihood (1–10) and cast your vote — one vote per threat, it locks in once cast.
  4. Once all three resolve, a 30-second Security Audit begins: the risk matrix disappears and a list of every connected codename appears instead.
  5. Click a codename to vote to Revoke them. Whoever gets the most votes when the clock hits zero gets ejected.

Winning

  • If the room ejects the Insider Threat — Analysts win.
  • If an innocent Analyst gets ejected (or no one does) — the Insider Threat wins.
  • Getting ejected locks your screen with a full-screen CREDENTIALS REVOKED message. You'll still see how it all played out.

What to do

  • If you're the Insider Threat, nothing you submit looks any different — vote convincingly and blend in.
  • If you're an Analyst, watch how people vote across all three threats for anything that looks off, then make your case out loud before the audit clock runs out.
  • This is a social game first: the real detective work happens in conversation in the room, not in the UI.

Judging a threat (no security background needed)

Each threat has two things to rate, and they're independent questions:

  • Likelihood — how likely is this actually an attack, versus an innocent explanation (a forgotten password, a late backup job, someone traveling)?
  • Severity — if it is real, how bad would it be? A login spike on a personal account is low-stakes; a database being copied out is not.

Every threat also shows a plain-English hint under the technical description — it explains what's going on, not what to vote. The judgment call is still yours.

# Crit 9

**What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?**

Playtesting the Week 9 proof-of-life showed the real problem wasn't a
missing feature, it was missing urgency. Multiple threats sat open at once,
so the room never actually looked at the same thing together — it felt like
triaging a shared inbox, not watching a live incident. The generator was
also hallucinating: titles and descriptions picked different names for the
same alert (a.okafor in one line, j.santos in the next), which broke trust
in the data before anyone even got to vote on it. The breakthrough was
realizing both problems had the same fix: collapse everything down to one
shared clock and one shared threat, so there's nothing to look at except
what everyone else is looking at right now.

**What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?**

Committing to Focus Mode forced a harder call: what happens to someone who
connects with three seconds left on the timer? I decided to drop them
straight into the live vote rather than build a waiting room, even though
it means a panicked last-second vote is noisier data. I want to be a
developer who's willing to pick the feel over the spreadsheet-clean answer
when the brief is actually about chaos and shared stakes, not accuracy —
that's what makes "everyone fights the same clock, watching the same dots
move" actually true, instead of just a feature I added.
